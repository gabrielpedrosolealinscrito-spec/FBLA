## Slide 1 — Title + Team · Luke · 0:15

**L:** Good [morning / afternoon], judges. I'm Luke, this is Gabriel, and we built **Potential** — the first product that tells you where you'd actually thrive, and then hands you the roadmap to get there. Let me show you the problem we set out to solve.

---

## Slide 2 — Problem · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Picture this. You're twenty-four. You're deciding where to build your life — your career, your home, maybe even your country. And right now, you're doing it with five browser tabs open and zero real answers. Numbeo for cost of living. Reddit for opinions. Google for everything else. LinkedIn for jobs. And a whole lot of guesswork.

[PAUSE]

Not one product on the market connects who you are to where you should go. And according to the U.S. Census Bureau's Current Population Survey, twenty-five-to-thirty-four-year-olds move at roughly sixteen to eighteen percent every year — the highest mobility rate of any adult age group. This is a massive, recurring decision made with broken tools.

---

## Slide 3 — Market Opportunity · Luke · 0:55

**L:** So how big is that? Let me build it from the ground up.

Start with the top number. According to the Census Bureau's Current Population Survey, about **eleven million Americans** cross a county or state line every single year — that's the population actively making a destination decision. That's our total market.

Now narrow it. Roughly eighteen percent of those movers are aged twenty-two to thirty-four — our digital-native user — and according to Pew Research, ninety-nine percent of that age group owns a smartphone. That gives us a serviceable market of about **two million people a year**.

Capture just one percent of that over three years and you reach **sixty thousand paying users** — about six hundred to seven hundred thousand dollars in revenue — and that's a deliberately conservative penetration rate.

[Optional, only if verified ⚠️F1:] And the international signal is real too — MBO Partners reports about seventeen million Americans now identify as digital nomads.

---

## Slide 4 — Competitive Landscape · Luke · 0:40

**L:** You'd think someone owns this market. Someone almost did. **Teleport** — two hundred sixty-six cities, quiz-driven, well-funded — was the closest thing to what we're building. And in 2022, according to Topia's own press release, MOVE Guides acquired Teleport and folded it into an enterprise B2B platform. The consumer product is gone.

[PAUSE]

That's not a market that failed. That's a market that was abandoned. The demand was real enough to buy — and today no well-capitalized player is serving individual consumers. The others? Nomad List gives you city scores and a forum. WhereNext sells a seventy-nine-dollar static PDF. None of them give you a personalized roadmap, live data, or a visa pathway. The gap is wide open.

---

## Slide 5 — Solution + Value Proposition · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Here's what Potential does. You start with five browser tabs and zero answers. You finish — in a single run — with a ranked list of cities matched to you, a financial model showing exactly what your life looks like on your salary, and a step-by-step plan to actually get there.

And we meter it simply. **Free** gets you your number-one match — that's the hook. **Ninety-nine cents** unlocks the full financial picture for that city — less than a coffee. **Nine ninety-nine** — our most popular tier — gives you the full ranked list, the live-AI layer, and the relocation roadmap. **Twenty-nine ninety-nine** adds the visa concierge. And your runs never expire — you buy when you're ready.

---

## Slide 6 — Three Differentiators · Luke → Gabriel · 0:45

**L:** Three things no competitor has. **First — live AI.** Every other tool hands you a static data table. We generate a real-time picture of your life in that city: real job listings, real housing, a real day-in-the-life — at the moment you run it. **Second — the personalized roadmap.** WhereNext charges seventy-nine dollars for a static PDF; we generate a dynamic, six-section plan built around your citizenship, your timeline, and your finances.

And the third differentiator is where Gabriel comes in — because he's lived it.

[HANDOFF — L → G]

**G:** The visa concierge isn't a feature we bolted on — it comes from experience. I've navigated the F-1, OPT, and O-1A pathway myself. Our concierge screens your eligibility, compares pathways, and hands you off to a licensed attorney. It's informational, not legal advice — but it's authentic, and that credibility is something no competitor can copy overnight. Let me actually show you the product.

## Slide 8 — Business Model + Pricing · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Relocation is a one-time decision. Charging a monthly subscription for a choice that happens once would destroy trust — so we don't. Instead we modeled this on **16Personalities** — over a hundred million test-takers, built on one-time run credits that never expire, no subscription. We mirror that structure exactly. ⚠️F4

Free, ninety-nine cents, nine ninety-nine, twenty-nine ninety-nine. The ninety-nine-cent entry is almost frictionless — and once someone's paid once, the jump to our nine-ninety-nine Plus tier is where the real value lands: the live data and the roadmap. And beyond the consumer product, we have two scaling revenue streams — affiliate referrals from our attorney network, and a future B2B employer-benefits product — the Teleport path, but run deliberately, after we've built the consumer moat first.

---

## Slide 9 — Financials: Model + Unit Economics · Gabriel · 0:55

**G:** Let me walk you through the model — and you can re-derive every number on this slide in about sixty seconds.

[Point to the chart] **We break even in Month 4.** On the full model — paying marketing and hosting every month, not just startup cost — cumulative net turns positive in Month 4. The simple check agrees: a thousand dollars of startup cost, divided by about nine dollars of net per paying user, is a hundred and twelve users — and we clear that in Month 4, at roughly a hundred sixty paid users.

Now the margin. According to Anthropic's published pricing, our API cost is about **six cents per run**. ⚠️F3 On a nine-ninety-nine Plus purchase, that's a **ninety-eight percent gross margin**. This is an AI-native product — no data pipeline, no servers — so the cost of serving one more user is near zero. By Month 12 we're at about eight thousand dollars cumulative net, and by Month 24, about thirty-four thousand.

---

## Slide 10 — Financials: LTV : CAC · Gabriel · 0:30

**G:** And the unit economics hold. Our blended customer acquisition cost is about **eight to twelve dollars** — modeled from named-channel benchmarks, and we state that as an assumption, not measured data. Our blended lifetime value is about **thirteen to fifteen dollars**, because this cohort relocates more than once in their twenties and thirties — and every move is a fresh purchase. ⚠️F7

[If challenged on the ratio:] "It's intentional. Relocation is a one-time decision — we earn repeat revenue through real life recurrence, not a subscription that breaks trust. Our moat isn't ARPU; it's the data and trust we build before the B2B layer."

[HANDOFF — G → L] **G:** The economics work. Now — how do we reach those two million people?

---

## Slide 11 — Marketing + Acquisition · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Organic-first, four channels, each one hitting people at the exact moment they're deciding to move. **Search** — someone typing "cost of living in Lisbon" already has the problem; that's our highest-intent channel. **Reddit** — communities like r/IWantOut exist precisely because our user already gathers there, and that's near-zero cost. **TikTok and Reels** — Gabriel's immigration journey is the content; authentic, organic, and free to make. And **university career centers** — reaching F-1 and graduating students facing "where next" the moment they leave school.

Blend it together and our customer acquisition cost is that eight-to-twelve dollars — and the whole model is built so any assumption can be stress-tested on the spot.

---

## Slide 12 — The Ask + Vision · Luke · 0:30

**L:** So here's where we stand. The gap is real — the market leader exited, eleven million people a year are still making this decision, and no one is serving them. The model works — break-even in Month 4, ninety-eight percent margins, positive unit economics from the start.

Potential is ready to be the first product that actually answers the question every person in this room has faced: *where should I build my life?* We've built it. We're ready to grow it. Thank you.

[End on eye contact — not the slide.]
