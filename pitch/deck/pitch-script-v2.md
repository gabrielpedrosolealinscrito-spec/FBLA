## Slide 1 — Title + Team · Luke · 0:15

**L:** Good [morning / afternoon], judges. We're Luke and Gabe, and we built **Potential** — a product that's **live right now** and answers a question almost everyone in this room has faced: *where should I build my life?* Let me show you why that question is so broken today.

---

## Slide 2 — Problem · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Picture yourself at twenty-four. You're deciding where to build your life — your career, your home, maybe even your country. And you're doing it with five browser tabs open and zero real answers. Numbeo for cost of living. Reddit for opinions. LinkedIn for jobs. Google for the rest. And a whole lot of guesswork.

[PAUSE]

Not one product connects *who you are* to *where you should go.* And this isn't a rare decision — according to the U.S. Census Bureau's Current Population Survey, twenty-five-to-thirty-four-year-olds move at roughly **sixteen to eighteen percent every year**, the highest rate of any adult age group. It's a massive, recurring, life-shaping decision made with broken tools.

---

## Slide 3 — Market Opportunity · Luke · 0:55

**L:** So how big is that? Let me build it from the ground up.

According to the Census Bureau, about **eleven million Americans** cross a county or state line every year — that's the population actively making a destination decision. That's our total market.

Now narrow it. Roughly eighteen percent of those movers are twenty-two to thirty-four — our digital-native user — and according to Pew Research, ninety-nine percent of them own a smartphone. That's a serviceable market of about **two million people a year.**

[PAUSE]

Capture just **one percent** over three years and that's **sixty thousand paying users** — six to seven hundred thousand dollars — at a deliberately conservative penetration rate.

[Optional, only if verified ⚠️F1:] And the appetite is global — MBO Partners reports about seventeen million Americans now identify as digital nomads.

---

## Slide 4 — Competitive Landscape · Luke · 0:40

**L:** You'd think someone owns this. Someone almost did. **Teleport** — two hundred sixty-six cities, quiz-driven, well-funded — was the closest thing to what we're building. And in 2022, according to Topia's own press release, MOVE Guides acquired Teleport and folded it into an enterprise B2B platform. The consumer product is **gone.**

[PAUSE]

That's not a market that failed. That's a market that got *abandoned.* The demand was real enough to buy — and today, no well-funded player serves the individual. The others don't fill the gap: Nomad List gives you city scores and a forum. WhereNext gives you free data with no plan attached — no profile, no roadmap, no live picture of your life, no visa path. The lane is wide open.

---

## Slide 5 — Solution + Value Proposition · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Here's what Potential does. You start with five tabs and zero answers. You finish — in a single run — with a ranked list of cities matched to *you*, a financial model showing exactly what your life looks like on *your* salary, and a step-by-step plan to actually get there.

That's the promise: **know where you'd thrive, and know how to get there.**

And we price it simply. **Free** gives you your number-one match — the hook. **Ninety-nine cents** unlocks the full financial picture — less than a coffee. **Nine ninety-nine**, our most popular tier, unlocks the full ranked list, the live-AI layer, and the relocation roadmap. **Twenty-nine ninety-nine** adds the visa concierge. And your runs never expire — you buy when you're ready.

---

## Slide 6 — Three Differentiators · Luke → Gabe · 0:45

**L:** Three things no competitor has. **First, live AI** — every other tool hands you a static table; we generate a real-time picture of your life in that city: real jobs, real housing, a real day-in-the-life, at the moment you run it. **Second, the personalized roadmap** — WhereNext sells a seventy-nine-dollar static PDF; ⚠️F6 we build a dynamic, six-part plan around your citizenship, your timeline, your finances.

And the third one is personal — so I'll let Gabe tell it.

[HANDOFF — L → G. Gabe steps in, makes eye contact with the judges, slows down.]

**G:** The visa concierge isn't a feature we added. It's the reason I'm standing here. Three years ago I was an international student on an F-1 visa, trying to figure out not just where I'd thrive — but where I was even *allowed* to build a life. I lived the O-1A, OPT, H-1B maze. Our concierge screens your eligibility, compares your pathways, and hands you to a licensed attorney. It's information, not legal advice — but it's *authentic*, and that's something no competitor copies overnight. Let me show you the whole thing working.

## Slide 8 — Business Model + Pricing · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Relocation is a one-time decision. Charging a monthly subscription for a choice that happens once would destroy trust — so we don't. We modeled this on **16Personalities** — over a hundred million test-takers, one-time credits that never expire, no consumer subscription. ⚠️F4 We mirror that exactly.

Free, ninety-nine cents, nine ninety-nine, twenty-nine ninety-nine. The ninety-nine-cent door is almost frictionless — and once someone's paid once, the jump to our nine-ninety-nine Plus tier is where the real value lands: the live data and the roadmap. Beyond the consumer product, two more revenue streams — affiliate referrals from our attorney network, and a future B2B employer-benefits product. The Teleport path — run deliberately, after we've built the consumer moat first.

---

## Slide 9 — Financials: Model + Margin · Gabe · 0:55

**G:** Let me walk the model — and you can re-derive every number on this slide in about sixty seconds.

[Point to chart] **We break even in Month 4** — on the full model, paying marketing and hosting every month, not just startup cost. The simple check agrees: a thousand dollars of startup cost, over about nine dollars of net per paying user, is a hundred twelve users — and we clear that in Month 4, at roughly a hundred sixty.

[PAUSE]

Now the margin. According to Anthropic's published pricing, our cost is about **six cents per run.** ⚠️F3 On a nine-ninety-nine Plus purchase, that's a **ninety-eight percent gross margin.** This is an AI-native product — no data pipeline, no servers — so serving one more user costs almost nothing. By Month 12 we're at about eight thousand dollars cumulative net; by Month 24, about thirty-four thousand.

---

## Slide 10 — Financials: LTV : CAC · Gabe · 0:35

**G:** And the unit economics hold. Blended customer acquisition cost is about **eight to twelve dollars** — modeled from named-channel benchmarks, and we state that as an assumption, not measured data. Blended lifetime value is about **thirteen to fifteen dollars.** ⚠️F7

Now — you'll notice that's about a **one-point-four to one** ratio. If we were a subscription SaaS, that number would scare me. We're not, and that's the point. Relocation is a once-a-decade decision; charging monthly would destroy trust. So we built for **profitability by Month 4** and repeat revenue from real life-recurrence — plus an affiliate and B2B layer that lift that ratio *without spending another dollar to acquire anyone.* The lean ratio isn't a flaw. It's the honest shape of a one-time-decision business done right.

[HANDOFF — G → L] **G:** "The economics work. Now — how do we reach those two million people?"

---

## Slide 11 — Marketing + Acquisition · Luke · 0:40

**L:** Organic-first. Four channels, each catching people at the exact moment they decide to move. **Search** — someone typing "cost of living in Lisbon" already has the problem; highest intent. **Reddit** — communities like r/IWantOut exist *because* our user already gathers there; near-zero cost, and it's where we ran our own launch. **TikTok and Reels** — Gabe's immigration journey is the content; authentic and free to make. And **university career centers** — reaching F-1 and graduating students at the "where next" moment. ⚠️F5

Blend it, and CAC is that eight-to-twelve dollars — and the whole model is built so any assumption can be stress-tested on the spot.

---

## Slide 12 — The Ask + Vision · Luke · 0:35

**L:** So here's where we stand. The gap is real — the market leader walked away, eleven million people a year still make this decision, and no one's serving them. The product is **built and live.** And the model works — we break even in Month 4 on about **a thousand dollars** of startup cost, at ninety-eight percent margins.

[PAUSE]

So notice what we're *not* asking for. We don't need venture capital — we built a profitable product for the price of a used laptop. What we need is **distribution**: university career centers to put Potential in front of graduating students, and an attorney network to power the visa concierge. Help us reach the two million people a year who need this — we built the answer, it's running today, and we're ready to grow it.

**Potential: know where you'd thrive — and how to get there.** Thank you.

[End on eye contact. Not the slide.]
