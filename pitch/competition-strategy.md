## 1. How advancement actually works (and what it means for us)

From the official guidelines:

- **Prelim score only decides advancement.** It is NOT carried into finals.
- **Finals score alone determines final rank** and the trophy.
- Number advancing per section depends on how many sections there are:
  - 2 sections → top 6 each · 3 → top 4 · 4 → top 3 · 5 → top 3 · **>5 sections → top 2 each.**

**Strategic implications:**

1. **There is no sandbagging.** Because prelim points don't carry, you cannot "save" your best run for finals. You must be **top ~2–3 of your section on Sunday** or there is no Monday. Go full power in prelims.
2. **You peak twice.** Sunday to advance, Monday to win. Same format, fresh slate. Build the plan so the team can deliver the A-game *twice* with a night in between — that's a stamina and nerves problem, not a content problem.
3. **Entrepreneurship Pitch is a popular event → likely many sections → likely top 2 advance.** Plan for the hardest case: you need to be one of the **two best in your room**. Treat Sunday as a real fight, not a checkpoint.

## 2. Judge psychology (who scores you, what moves them)

Judges are business professionals/educators scoring against NACE competencies (Communication, Critical Thinking, Professionalism, Career/Self-Development). For a *pitch* specifically, the rubric's top band rewards:

- **Persuasion:** "compelling, confident, highly persuasive… strong call to action… creates excitement and belief in the business's success." → Conviction is scored. Flat = mid.
- **Q&A:** "responds confidently with clear, accurate, thoughtful answers that *enhance* the presentation." → Q&A is a scoring opportunity, not a survival exercise. A great answer earns points the pitch couldn't.
- **Delivery:** logical flow + business language + all 4 of {confidence, body language, eye contact, voice projection}.

**What judges reward:** defensible numbers, a founder they believe, composure under a hard question, a clear "why us."
**What judges punish:** hand-waving, reading slides, defensiveness, numbers that don't add up, and a team where one person dominates while the other stands there.

**Our unfair advantage to lean on:** a *real working product* + a founder with *real lived immigration expertise* + *sourced* numbers. That trio is rare in a student pitch field and maps directly onto "exceeds expectations." Most entries are either a polished deck with no product, or a vague app with a hand-waved market. We are neither — **so the whole game is not letting nerves, a broken demo, or a fumbled Q&A bury the edge we already have.**

## 3. Top vulnerabilities + contingencies (ranked by danger)

### V1 — The demo is the single biggest risk. (CRITICAL)
Product is Phase 2 of 8 with 6 days left. A live-AI demo that stutters, spins, or fails in front of judges turns our centerpiece into a liability.
- **Mitigation already designed:** golden-path cache fallback (renders instantly on hotspot kill) + the framing "the demo proves it's real; it doesn't carry the score" (4 of 6 content dimensions live in the deck).
- **DECISION GATE — Saturday June 6:** If the live demo is not *reliably* clean by Saturday rehearsal, **cut to a scripted screenshot/recorded walkthrough.** A confident screenshot tour beats a nervous live failure every time. This is a pre-committed decision so nobody panics at the table Sunday morning. Do not gamble the centerpiece on an unrehearsed live call.

### V2 — LTV:CAC is thin. This is the sharpest knife a judge can use. (HIGH)
CAC $8–12, LTV $13–15 → ratio ~1.2–1.3x. Investors/judges who know the space expect **3:1+**. A sharp judge: *"Your lifetime value barely exceeds your acquisition cost — that's not a scalable business."* The current Q&A answer hand-waves this.
- **Tighten the answer to a crisp, pre-rehearsed reframe:** "On the *consumer* one-time product, yes, the ratio is deliberately lean and still profitable from Month 4 — we're not buying growth at a loss. The 3:1 comes from two places the consumer number doesn't capture: life-recurrence (this cohort relocates 2–3x in their 20s–30s, every move a fresh purchase) and the affiliate + future B2B layer that monetizes the same user without new CAC. We optimized for profitability and trust first, blitz-scale second." Drill this until it's reflexive. **This is the most likely question to lose us points if fumbled, and the most likely to *win* points if nailed.**

### V3 — "Isn't this just a student/one-person project?" (HIGH)
Judges may not believe a 2-person team executes this for real. Q20 covers scale mechanically; the *emotional* answer is Gabriel's authentic F-1 → OPT → O-1A journey.
- **Make founder authenticity MORE prominent, not less.** It's the one asset no competitor and no judge skepticism can erase. The visa concierge isn't a feature — it's *why this team specifically* gets to build this. Lead Slide 6 handoff with conviction, not as a bullet.

### V4 — Pricing is complex for a 10-minute pitch. (MEDIUM)
Four tiers + never-expiring runs + affiliate + future B2B is a lot of surface area. The Business Model row suffers if it feels convoluted.
- **Anchor on ONE number: the $9.99 Plus tier** — that's where the value and the money are. $0.99 is "the frictionless door," $29.99 is "the serious-mover upgrade," B2B is "later." Say it as a ladder, not a menu. If short on time, the marketing slide compresses first (already in the protocol checklist) — but never let the pricing get muddy.

### V5 — SOM looks small for a "venture." (MEDIUM)
60K users / ~$600–700K over 3 years reads modest. A judge: *"That's a lifestyle business, not a venture."*
- **Answer:** "That's the beachhead — deliberately conservative 1% penetration on a tightly-scoped, *defensible* market. The venture scale is the B2B employer-relocation-benefits product the consumer data unlocks — the exact path Teleport took, but we build the consumer moat first." Reframes 'small' as 'disciplined.'

### V6 — Team balance / all-must-participate. (LOW but binary-ish)
Rules require all members actively participate. Watch that Gabe isn't "the tech guy who only talks during the demo." His share (demo + financials + ~half of Q&A) is adequate — keep it that way and make sure both make eye contact and own their sections with presence.
