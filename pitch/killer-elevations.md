## The judge's-eye problem with the current pitch

It's *correct* but it's *generic in shape*: problem → market → solution → ask, narrated competently. Every strong team in that room will have roughly that shape. The rubric's top band rewards **"compelling, confident, highly persuasive… creates excitement and belief."** Excitement doesn't come from a clean TAM slide. It comes from **a story judges feel, a line they remember, and a founder they believe.** We have the single best raw material in the room for all three — Gabriel's real immigration journey — and right now it's buried as bullet #3 on slide 6.

## ELEVATION 6 — A rehearsed Q&A tag-team protocol (2-person teams lose points here)

Two-person Q&A fails in predictable ways: both hesitate, both talk at once, or the partner contradicts/repeats. Rehearse this:

1. **Primary answers** (per routing in `qa-bank.md`). Starts within 2 seconds — never let dead air sit.
2. **Partner adds AT MOST one sentence**, only if it strengthens. Never repeat, never contradict.
3. **Rescue signal:** if primary stalls, partner says *"Let me take that"* — agreed in advance, looks seamless.
4. **Land every answer.** End on a period and eye contact, never a trailing "…so, yeah." A crisp stop reads as confidence.
5. **If you don't know:** "Great question — we haven't modeled that yet, but here's how we'd approach it…" Never bluff a number. Judges smell it.
